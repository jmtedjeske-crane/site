AI and Legal Research

AI and Legal Research is a one-credit, seven-week experiential course that Julie is teaching at Penn State Dickinson Law for the first time in the fall 2026 term.

In progress Links to the course materials will appear on this page as Julie publishes them.

Three Roles for the Human in the Loop

The course organizes its exercises around three roles that a researcher plays when working with AI tools. The roles come from Julie’s article Teaching Legal Research Students to Be the “Human in the Loop”, which Perspectives: Teaching Legal Research and Writing published in 2026. Each week names one lead role, and the capstone project requires students to perform all three roles.

  • StrategistSecondary role: Translator
  • AnalystSecondary role: Scout
  • Decision-MakerSecondary role: Communicator

Goal: Teach students to have an ownership mindset and to aim for excellence, not just to avoid sanctions.

Assignments

  • Final Capstone Project

    45%

    Students complete a research project through a step-by-step process that ties back to the in-class exercises.

  • Weekly AI Updates

    20%

    Students complete four of five weekly updates on developments in legal AI. Pass/fail.

  • AI Deep Dive

    20%

    Each student chooses a book or an online course about AI from Julie’s list and studies it in depth. Pass/fail.

  • In-Class Work

    15%

    In-class exercises and the Week 7 certification. Pass/fail.

Weekly Plan

  • Week 1Oct. 19

    How Generative AI Works, and Prompting for Legal Research

    Lectures on generative AI, retrieval-augmented generation, and legal prompting. Students draft prompts for a family law fact pattern and test the prompts in CoCounsel, Harvey, Lexis Protégé, and a general chatbot.

  • Week 2Oct. 26

    Confidentiality, Data Scrubbing, and Professional Responsibility

    Students remove confidential client information from a document before they prompt an AI tool, and then work through a guided prompting exercise.

  • Week 3Nov. 2

    Hallucinations and Verification

    Students check a brief by hand against a verification checklist, and then check the same brief with brief-analysis software.

  • Week 4Nov. 9

    AI Compared with Secondary Sources

    Students compare an AI tool’s answer with a secondary source on the same question. Students then apply Mike Caulfield’s SIFT method to the empirical claims in a public-policy topic of their choice.

  • Week 5Nov. 16

    Analytics and Current Awareness

    Students compare the litigation analytics in Westlaw, Lexis, and Bloomberg Law with AI-generated content.

  • Week 6Nov. 23

    Statutory Research

    Students compare an AI tool’s statutory research against an annotated code, and then complete a state law survey.

  • Week 7Nov. 30

    AI Certification (asynchronous)

    Students complete either the Harvey Academy Legal Engineering Certification or the Clio Legal AI Fundamentals Certification.
