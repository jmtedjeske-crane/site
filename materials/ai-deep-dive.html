AI Deep Dive Assignment

AI Deep Dive

Key Takeaways

  • Choose one of four options: (1) a designated online course, (2) a book, (3) four episodes of the podcast The Last Invention, or (4) a short paper on a topic of your choice.
  • If your choice needs approval, email your request by [time] on [date].
  • Submit your finished work by [time] on [date].
  • Write between 500 and 750 words. This will be a reflection for Options 1 to 3 or the paper itself for Option 4.
  • The Deep Dive is graded pass/fail and is worth 20% of your course grade.
  • If you choose a book, you must get your own copy.

Purpose

This assignment is based on the recognition that students come to this course with different backgrounds in AI and different interests. The goal is to provide space and credit to do something that interests you.

Grading

The Deep Dive is pass/fail and is worth 20% of your course grade. I record a submission that passes on time at 95% of its value. If your first submission is unsatisfactory or late, you get one chance to resubmit. I record a satisfactory resubmission, or satisfactory work submitted late without an excuse, at 85% of its value.

Deadlines

Step Deadline
Email your request for approval (if needed) [time], [date] (Week 2)
Submit your finished work [time], [date] (Week 5)

The assignment opens in Week 1, and the finished work is due four weeks later.

Task

  1. Choose one option. The four options are described in the next section.
  2. Request approval by [date], if needed. You need advance approval only if you are reading a book not on the Suggested Book List or writing a short paper. Email your request to me at [your email address]. For a book, give the title and author. For a paper, describe your topic in two or three sentences.
  3. Complete the work. Finish the video course, the entire book, or all four podcast episodes. If you are writing a short paper, do the supporting research.
  4. Write your deliverable. Your deliverable must be between 500 and 750 words.
    • For Options 1, 2, and 3, write a reflection that covers:
      • what you learned;
      • how it may help you in practice; and
      • your own observations, which may include disagreement with the author or the course.
    • For Option 4, the paper is your deliverable. You do not write a separate reflection.
  5. Submit your deliverable by [time] on [date].

The Four Options

Option 1: Complete an Online Course

Choose one of the following two free courses.

  • AI Competence and Law Practice for Law Students. The Practicing Law Institute (PLI) developed this 12-module course with Professor Daniel W. Linna Jr. of Northwestern Pritzker School of Law and released it in August 2026. The modules cover how generative AI and AI agents work, professional responsibility, prompt engineering, verifying AI output, and designing supervised AI workflows. Complete all 12 modules. 
  • Anthropic’s AI Fluency videos plus “AI on Campus.” Anthropic developed the AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations course with Professor Rick Dakan of Ringling College and Professor Joseph Feller of University College Cork. The course teaches a four-part framework for working with AI: delegation, description, discernment, and diligence. For this option, you must complete both of the following:
    • Watch every video in the AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations playlist.
    • Watch Anthropic’s video “AI on Campus.”

Option 2: Read a Book

Choose a book from the Suggested Book List at the end of these instructions, or propose another book about AI. The list offers suggestions and does not limit your choice, but you need advance approval by [date] for any book not on the list. You must get your own copy of the book.

Option 3: Listen to The Last Invention

The Last Invention is a podcast series from Longview. The series traces the history of AI and the competing predictions about AI’s future. Listen to four episodes of your choice.

Option 4: Write a Short Paper

Write a paper of 500 to 750 words, with citations to your sources, on an issue in the use of AI in legal research. Possible topics include hallucinations, agentic research workflows, court rules that require lawyers to disclose AI use, and cognitive offloading. I must approve your topic by [date].

For ideas on topics and readings, you may consult Professor Rebecca Fordon’s reading list for 21st Century Lawyering (Ohio State, Spring 2026). The reading list is for inspiration only; you do not have to choose a topic or a reading from it.

Your citations do not need to follow Bluebook format. Each citation must provide enough information for a reader to locate the source. The paper replaces the reflection, so you do not write both.

Using AI in Your Deep Dive

You must complete the course, read the book, or listen to the episodes yourself. You may not paste an AI tool’s summary of the course, the book, or the episodes into your reflection. You may use AI in the writing process and any AI product.

I suggest a two-step process. First, write a rough draft yourself, preferably in complete sentences, although an outline also works. Then ask an AI tool to help you refine the draft. Your final reflection or paper must give your own account of what you learned and your own observations.

Criteria

Your Deep Dive passes when it meets all of the following criteria:

  • If your choice needed approval, you emailed your request by [date], and I approved it.
  • You completed the whole option: every module or video of the course, the entire book, or four full podcast episodes. I do not track your progress except through the substance of your reflection, which should show that you did the assigned work.
  • Your deliverable should be between 500 and 750 words.
  • If you chose Option 1, 2, or 3, your reflection covers all three parts: what you learned, how it may help you in practice, and your own observations.
  • If you wrote a paper, it addresses the approved topic and cites its sources acceptably.
  • Your reflection or paper is your own work and is not an AI tool’s summary.
  • You submitted your deliverable by [time] on [date].

Suggested Book List

These titles are suggestions, not a complete list. A book that is not on this list requires my approval. Most of the books were published in 2023 or later. The list also keeps several earlier books, marked “pre-2023.”

A. Law, the Legal Profession, and the Future of Work

  • Artificial Intelligence and the Practice of Law in a Nutshell, Korin Munsterman (2026).
  • Tomorrow’s Lawyers: An Introduction to Your Future, Richard Susskind (3d ed. 2023).
  • The Reasonable Robot: Artificial Intelligence and the Law, Ryan Abbott (2020) (pre-2023).
  • Futureproof: 9 Rules for Humans in the Age of Automation, Kevin Roose (2021) (pre-2023).

B. AI, Society, and Human Dignity

  • Nexus: A Brief History of Information Networks from the Stone Age to AI, Yuval Noah Harari (2024).
  • Code Dependent: Living in the Shadow of AI, Madhumita Murgia (2024).
  • Magnifica Humanitas: On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence, Pope Leo XIV (2026). The Vatican publishes the text free online, and several publishers sell it in book form.
  • Artificial Intimacy: Who We Become When We Talk to Machines, Sherry Turkle (2026).
  • You Are Not a Gadget: A Manifesto, Jaron Lanier (2010) (pre-2023).

C. Algorithms, Bias, and Power

  • Weapons of Math Destruction: How Big Data Increases Inequality and Threatens Democracy, Cathy O’Neil (2016) (pre-2023).
  • Automating Inequality: How High-Tech Tools Profile, Police, and Punish the Poor, Virginia Eubanks (2018) (pre-2023).
  • Algorithms of Oppression: How Search Engines Reinforce Racism, Safiya Umoja Noble (2018) (pre-2023).
  • Atlas of AI: Power, Politics, and the Planetary Costs of Artificial Intelligence, Kate Crawford (2021) (pre-2023).

D. AI, Work, and the Industry

  • The Reverse Centaur’s Guide to Life After AI: How to Think About Artificial Intelligence Before It’s Too Late, Cory Doctorow (2026).
  • The AGI Chronicles: The Inside Story of the Race to Create an Artificial Superintelligence, Kevin Roose (2026). The book is published on October 6, 2026.
  • Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI, Ethan Mollick (2024).
  • Co-Existence: The Next Phase of AI, Ethan Mollick (2026). The book is published on October 20, 2026.
  • The Coming Wave: Technology, Power, and the Twenty-First Century’s Greatest Dilemma, Mustafa Suleyman (2023).
  • Empire of AI: Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman’s OpenAI, Karen Hao (2025).
  • Supremacy: AI, ChatGPT, and the Race That Will Change the World, Parmy Olson (2024).

E. The Debate over Risk and Benefit

  • If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies: Why Superhuman AI Would Kill Us All, Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares (2025).
  • These Strange New Minds: How AI Learned to Talk and What It Means, Christopher Summerfield (2025).
  • The Alignment Problem: Machine Learning and Human Values, Brian Christian (2020) (pre-2023).
  • Genesis: Artificial Intelligence, Hope, and the Human Spirit, Henry Kissinger, Craig Mundie, and Eric Schmidt (2024).
  • More Everything Forever: AI Overlords, Space Empires, and Silicon Valley's Crusade to Control the Fate of Humanity, Adam Becker (2025).
  • The Singularity Is Nearer: When We Merge with AI, Ray Kurzweil (2024).
