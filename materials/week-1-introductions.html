Week 1: Introductions Discussion

Students post this introduction to a Canvas discussion board before the first class. The page below shows [date] and [time] wherever a deadline belongs.

Week 1 · Before our first class on [date]

Introductions

Purpose

Your introduction lets your classmates and me get to know you before the course begins.

Your introduction also helps me plan the course. This is the first year that I have taught this course, so I do not yet know what experience with AI students bring to it. I will use your answers to decide how much background to cover.

Task

Post a short introduction to this discussion board by [time] on [date]. You may reply to your classmates' posts, but you do not have to.

Please answer the questions below. Questions 1, 2, 3, and 5 are required, and Question 4 is optional. Organize your post using the question headings, such as "Question 1: Who are you?", so that each answer appears under its own heading. A few sentences for each question are enough.

Technically, you may use AI to help you draft your introduction. Because the questions ask only about you and your own experience, however, I encourage you to write this post without AI.

Question 1: Who are you?

Tell us your name and your year in law school. Tell us which areas of law interest you, or what kind of work you hope to do after graduation.

Question 2: How have you used AI so far?

Which AI tools have you used, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Westlaw CoCounsel, or Lexis Protégé? In general, what have you used those tools for, in school, at work, or in your personal life? How useful do you think AI is for legal research, and why?

Question 3: How does AI fit into a typical day?

Explain how you use AI in a typical day. If you do not use AI every day, describe a typical week or month instead.

Question 4 (optional): How do you feel about AI?

How do you feel about AI in general? Are you a "doomer," an enthusiast, a pragmatist, a skeptic, or a bit of each? Explain your answer.

Question 5: Icebreaker

Share one of the following: your favorite book or movie, your favorite quote, or a fun fact about yourself.

Criteria

This introduction counts toward the In-Class Work component of your grade, which I grade pass/fail. Your introduction passes when it answers Questions 1, 2, 3, and 5 and you post it by the deadline. I do not grade the opinions that you express. I also do not grade your spelling or grammar unless errors make your answer hard to understand.
