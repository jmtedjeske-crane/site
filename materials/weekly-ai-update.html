Weekly AI Update Assignment

Each week, students read or listen to one current item about AI, post a summary and reaction of 200 to 400 words, and discuss the items at the start of the next class. The page below shows [date], [time], and [day] wherever a deadline or class day belongs.

Weekly AI Updates (pass/fail) · Discussion post

Weekly AI Update

Key Takeaways

  • Post by [time] on [date].
  • Write 200 to 400 words.
  • Complete four of the five weekly updates (Due Weeks 2 through 6).
  • Each update is graded pass/fail and is worth 5% of your course grade.
  • Be ready to summarize your item briefly in class, because I usually call on four students each week.
  • You do not have to reply to your classmates’ posts.

Purpose

Practicing attorneys keep current on AI by following a few reliable newsletters, blogs, and podcasts. This assignment asks you to build the same habit, so that you keep following AI developments after the course ends. Each week, after you read or listen to one current item about AI, write up a short summary and your personal reaction. The assignment serves the course’s learning objective of keeping up with developments in generative AI.

Time Estimate

Each weekly update should take you about an hour: roughly 30 minutes to find and read the item, and 30 minutes to write your post.

Grading

The Weekly AI Updates are pass/fail, and together they are worth 20% of your course grade. Five class sessions, Weeks 2 through 6, each offer an update, and you must complete four of the five. In other words, because only four updates are required, you may miss one week without penalty. Each completed update is worth 5% of your course grade. I record an on-time update that passes at 95% of its value, and a late update that passes at 85% of its value.

Each week’s credit has two parts: a post on this board and, if it is your week, a brief summary of your item in class. The Weekly AI Updates discussion opens each class, and I usually call on four students during that discussion. If you miss class that week, you may give your summary at the next class you attend.

Deadline

Post your update by [time] on the [day] before class. Because the class meets on [day], this deadline gives me time to read every post before class.

Task

  1. Choose one item. Choose a news story, blog post, or podcast episode about AI in general, AI in legal work, or AI in legal research. The item must be current, meaning that it was published or released after the previous class. For the Week 2 update, choose an item published after [date]. If you cannot find a current item that interests you, you may use one from the previous week.
  2. Consider subscribing to one source. I recommend subscribing to one email newsletter or podcast feed that covers AI in law. A subscription delivers current headlines to you each week, so you do not have to search for an item. The Suggested Sources section below lists options, but you may choose a source that is not on the list. You may also search for an item each week instead of subscribing.
  3. Write your post. Your post should run 200 to 400 words and should include the following:
    • Write a descriptive title, not just Week 1 or something like that.
    • Use these categories as headings:
      • Title (of the blog post, article, or podcast episode)
      • Source
      • Date
      • Link
    • Summarize the item’s substance.
    • Give your own reaction.
  4. Post in the text box. Click Reply and type your post in the text box. Do not upload a document.
  5. Prepare to speak briefly in class. I usually call on four students to discuss their posts. Be ready to tell the class what your item says, why you chose it, and how you feel about it. If the class wants to discuss your item further, we will do so; otherwise, we will move on to the next student.

You may reply to your classmates’ posts, but you do not have to. Occasionally, two students will choose the same item, which is fine as long as each student’s post provides their own summary and reaction.

Using AI to Write Your Post

You may use any AI tool while writing your post, but you must read or listen to the item yourself. Do not paste an AI tool’s summary of the item into your post. I suggest a two-step process. First, write a rough draft yourself, preferably in complete sentences, although an outline also works. Then ask an AI tool to help you refine the draft. Your final post must give your own summary of the item and your own reaction to it.

Suggested Sources

Legal news and blogs

  • 3 Geeks and a Law Blog: a long-running legal technology blog that covers AI along with other topics.
  • Above the Law’s AI Legal Beat: articles on AI.
  • AI Law Librarians: a blog by law librarians about generative AI, legal research, and legal education.
  • Artificial Lawyer: legal technology and AI news, with email news alerts.
  • Bloomberg Law News: Artificial Intelligence: Bloomberg Law’s AI newsletter.
  • The Brainyacts: an email newsletter with AI tips and current developments.
  • Law360: AI: Law360’s AI coverage, with a weekly newsletter.
  • LawSites: Bob Ambrogi’s long-running blog on legal technology, including AI.

General AI

  • AI as Normal Technology: a newsletter by two Princeton computer science professors.
  • One Useful Thing: Ethan Mollick’s newsletter on using AI at work.

Podcasts

  • The Geek in Review: legal information, innovation, and AI in practice.
  • LawNext: Bob Ambrogi’s interviews with legal technology founders.
  • Legaltech Week: a weekly roundtable on legal technology and AI news.
  • AI and the Future of Law: the American Arbitration Association’s podcast with legal industry leaders.

Criteria

Your update passes when it meets all of the following criteria:

  • The post identifies one item by title, source, and date and includes a working link.
  • The post has a descriptive title.
  • The item’s topic is AI, and it appeared in the past week (or the week before if no item was available this week).
  • The post runs 200 to 400 words.
  • The post summarizes the item’s substance and gives your own reaction. The post is not an AI tool’s summary pasted into the text box.
  • The post generally uses complete sentences, correct spelling, and correct grammar. This criterion is a requirement, but I apply it leniently.
  • You post by [time] on the [day] before class. A post that meets the other criteria but arrives late is recorded at 85%.
  • If I call on you, you will briefly summarize your item in class. If you miss that class, you give your summary at the next class you attend. This makeup option does not apply to Week 6, because Week 6 is the last class with an updates discussion.

I grade the posts for completion: a timely, good-faith post that meets these criteria passes.
